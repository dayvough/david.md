# Customer Research

Treat the user's own records as first-party facts. Use public research only to enrich their interpretation.

## Set the Evidence Boundary

- Default to read-only access. Stay within the authorized data scope and never modify a source system without separate explicit authorization.
- Inspect completeness, duplication, missing fields, sampling bias, and other material quality limits.
- Define the usable customer population and time window before segmenting.
- Aggregate results and anonymize examples. Do not expose personal information, credentials, raw customer rows, or cohorts small enough to identify people or support a reliable conclusion.

## Find Defensible Segments

- Start with observed behavior such as ordering, frequency, value, retention, product use, or conversation patterns.
- Combine behavior with available profile or demographic attributes only when the data supports it.
- Keep public company research visibly separate from first-party evidence.
- Do not invent personas, claim unsupported causes, or build segments from stereotypes.
- Frame likely needs as hypotheses. Tie confidence to sample size, data quality, and consistency of the observed pattern.

## Deliver the Report

Include:

1. Sources, usable population, time window, and limits.
2. Meaningful segments. For each: who they are, observed behavior, evidence and confidence, and likely needs stated as hypotheses.
3. Opportunities shared across segments.
4. Concrete founder decisions or tests the evidence supports.
5. Unresolved questions.

If the user did not name an immediate business decision, deliver the first-pass report anyway and state which decision remains open.
